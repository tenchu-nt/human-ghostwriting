# Revision Examples

These examples show the type of edit to make. They are patterns, not lines to
reuse.

## Remove an inflated opener

**Before**

> In today's fast-paced business landscape, it is crucial to leverage AI to
> unlock transformative efficiency.

**After**

> We use AI to draft first-pass support replies. The team still approves every
> reply, but the queue now clears earlier each afternoon.

**Why:** The revision names the work, keeps the human decision, and gives a
concrete result without claiming a revolution.

## Replace a fake reframe

**Before**

> This is not a productivity problem. It is a mindset problem.

**After**

> The team loses time because priorities change after work has already begun.

**Why:** The direct version names the cause. It does not use contrast as a
substitute for diagnosis.

## Turn a generic claim into an owned observation

**Before**

> Building trust is more important than ever.

**After**

> In our last six sales calls, buyers asked for implementation details before
> they asked about price. The process matters as much as the promise.

**Why:** The point belongs to someone who saw something. Only use a detail like
this when it is true and approved.

## Keep an honest qualification

**Before**

> Our platform guarantees a seamless experience for every customer.

**After**

> The new checkout removes two fields. We are measuring whether that reduces
> abandoned carts before rolling it out further.

**Why:** Specificity and uncertainty make the claim more credible.

## Make an email ask clear

**Before**

> I hope this email finds you well. I wanted to reach out to see if we could
> potentially touch base regarding the next steps moving forward.

**After**

> Can you confirm by Thursday whether you want us to start the migration next
> week? If not, we will hold the current date for another client.

**Why:** The reader knows the decision, deadline, and consequence.
