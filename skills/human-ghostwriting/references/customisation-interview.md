# Human Ghostwriting: Eight-Question Customisation Interview

Use this optional guide when you want a reusable author profile or more
consistent voice across future work. You can answer any number of prompts;
short answers and "not sure" are fine. Do not share private client information,
passwords, unpublished financial information, or anything you do not want an
AI to process.

If you answer any prompts, paste or upload them with a request. The skill can
turn them into an author profile and use it as evidence for future drafts.

## 1. Who is writing, and who are they writing to?

Give your role, the usual reader, and the relationship. For example: "I run a
small design studio. I write to prospective clients and current clients."

## 2. What should your writing usually achieve?

State the outcome you want most often: make a decision, explain, sell, build
trust, teach, update, challenge, or something else. Include a typical call to
action if you use one.

## 3. What are two or three examples of your writing that sound right?

Paste short approved samples or link to material you own and can share. Say
what each sample gets right. Samples are evidence of preference, not text to
copy.

## 4. How should the writing feel?

Choose a few useful contrasts: direct or diplomatic, concise or explanatory,
formal or conversational, warm or neutral, playful or serious. Mention the
regional spelling or language to use.

## 5. What words, phrases, habits, or formats are naturally yours?

List expressions you genuinely use, your preferred greeting or sign-off, how
you use lists or headings, and any habits that make your writing recognisable.

## 6. What should the skill avoid?

List words, phrases, tones, claims, punctuation, formatting, and stereotypes
that would make the draft feel wrong. Include examples of writing you dislike.

## 7. What proof can the writing use, and what must it never invent?

List facts, types of evidence, outcomes, experiences, or numbers you can
stand behind. State approval boundaries for client names, results, opinions,
and sensitive subjects.

**Answer:**

## 8. Which formats and constraints matter most?

Name the channels you use, preferred length, accessibility needs, industry or
legal requirements, and anything that changes by audience. For example:
"Client emails are plain text and under 180 words. LinkedIn posts can be more
personal."

## What happens next

The skill converts your answers into a working author profile. Review that
profile before relying on it for important writing. Correct it whenever your
voice, audience, or boundaries change.
